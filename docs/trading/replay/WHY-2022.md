# Why did the strategy stop working after 2022?

**Date:** 2026-08-12 · Diagnostic only — no parameter, gate, threshold or flag changed
**Basis:** 4,025 session records + 765 Gate 2 candidates + 403 executable-stop trades, 0 guard violations
**Data:** [`regime/sessions.ndjson`](regime/sessions.ndjson) · [`regime/breadth.ndjson`](regime/breadth.ndjson) · [`regime/leaders.json`](regime/leaders.json)

---

## The answer

**The strategy did not break in any way this analysis can find. What changed is
the environment it was harvesting.**

| | 2015–2021 | 2022–2026 |
|---|---|---|
| VN-Index | **+15.5%/yr** | +3.7%/yr |
| **Median replayable stock, annualised** | **+13.7%/yr** | **−4.2%/yr** |
| **Share positive over the period** | **87.4%** | **41.2%** |

The index kept rising while the median stock it contains fell — a narrow market.
And it is not a population artefact. **Paired on a fixed cohort**, which removes the objection that the two periods
contain different symbols — all 261 replayable names from the first period are
present in the second:

| fixed cohort (n=261) | median total | annualised | share positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | +145.5% | +13.7%/yr | 87.4% |
| 2022–2026 | −17.4% | −4.1%/yr | 41.8% |

**214 of 261 (82.0%) of the very same symbols earned less per year in the second
period.** That is a paired comparison, not two population averages placed side by
side.

In the first period a long-only continuation system could hardly fail: 87% of
stocks rose. In the second, fewer than half did. This system has no short side
and no market-neutral component, so much of what it earned was drift.

*What this does and does not establish:* it establishes that replayable names
drifted up in the first period and not the second. It does not establish a
market-wide claim — delisted names are absent from the database entirely, and
their absence flatters the second period, so the true figure is likely worse.

Everything else below is consistent with that and adds one mechanical detail:
**setups still form, still look identical, and still go against you by the same
amount — they simply stop running.**

## What did NOT change (the negative results matter most)

### Q1 — Market regime, as Gate 1 measures it: **unchanged**

| | sessions | PASS | WARNING | FAIL | bullish | momentum up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 2,879 | 22.2% | 65.7% | 12.1% | 59.7% | 30.6% |
| 2022–2026 | 1,146 | 22.5% | 66.0% | 11.5% | 60.3% | 30.8% |

Every figure matches to within 0.6pp. **This is the most informative result in the
document**, because of *why* it is unchanged: Gate 1 reads the index alone, and
the index is precisely the thing that did not deteriorate. It kept rising
(+3.7%/yr) while the median stock underneath it fell 4.2%/yr — a narrow,
large-cap-led market looks identical to a broad one through an index-only lens.

Gate 1 was not wrong about the index. It was measuring something that stayed
healthy while the tradable population beneath it did not — and it has no term
that could have registered the difference.

*Stated carefully:* on its own this is an implementation fact, not a cause. It
carries weight only in combination with the paired-cohort evidence above, which
is what shows the opportunity set deteriorated while the index stayed investable.

### Q5 — Entry extension: **unchanged**

| | n | p25 | median | p75 | mean | share >3% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 376 | 0.42% | 1.06% | 2.14% | 1.40% | 11.4% |
| 2022–2026 | 389 | 0.48% | 1.15% | 2.04% | 1.42% | 12.3% |

Entries are not being chased later than before.

### Q3 — Breakout→pullback geometry: **unchanged where it counts**

| | zone width | position in zone | stop distance | **stop/ATR** | ATR % of price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 1.95% | 1.67 | 4.21% | 1.61 | 2.67% |
| 2022–2026 | 1.95% | 1.66 | 4.37% | 1.50 | **3.12%** |

Stock-level volatility rose 17%, and the structural stop widened only 3.8%, so on
the candidate population the stop shrank from 1.61 to 1.50 ATR. **But on the
trades actually taken, stop/ATR is 1.73 in both periods** — identical. The
volatility drift did not reach the executed book.

### Q7 — Setup frequency: **did fall, but not as a clean break**

Candidates per 1,000 symbol-sessions: 6.23 (2015–21) → 4.48 (2022–26). But by
year: 2018 5.55, 2021 5.56, **2023 6.07**, 2024 5.06, 2025 4.58, 2022 3.68,
2026 2.50 (partial).

The aggregate decline is real — 2022, 2025 and 2026 all sit below the pre-period
rate. But 2023 exceeds several pre-break years, so this is not a step change, and
pointing only at 2023 would be special pleading. **The defensible reading: setups
did become somewhat scarcer, unevenly, and this is not the primary mechanism** —
the years the strategy lost money most consistently (2023, 2024) are not the years
setups were scarcest.

Rank score is flat (median 2231.6 → 2211.3) and Tier A share rose slightly
(56.6% → 61.2%): the setups that did form were not lower-quality by the scanner's
own measure.

### Q8 — Breadth: **normal in 2023–2025**

| | median % above MA50 |
|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 49.7% |
| 2022–2026 | 44.1% |

By year: 2021 **67.0** (exceptional), 2022 35.1, 2023 51.3, 2024 49.7, 2025 49.1,
2026 37.1. Short-horizon breadth in 2023–2025 is indistinguishable from
2015–2019. It does not explain losses in those years.

*Treat this series as colour, not as a clean statistic:* it is computed over every
symbol with 50 prior bars, not over the point-in-time tradable universe, and the
sample grows from 152 to 291 symbols across the window. It uses only bars at or
before each date, so no future prices leak, but the composition is not constant.

Note the contrast with the cumulative figure: 44% of stocks are above their MA50
on a typical day, yet only 41.8% of the fixed cohort were up **over the whole
period**. Day-to-day
participation stayed normal; sustained advance did not.

### Q10 — Post-2022 winners are **not** a shape the scanner newly misses

| | top-30 leaders forming a Gate 2 candidate | ever surfaced |
|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 13/30 | 9/30 |
| 2022–2026 | 9/30 | 9/30 |

Coverage of market leaders is about 30% in **both** eras — the scanner always
missed roughly seven of every ten leaders, mostly small illiquid names that
tradability filters exclude by design. There is no new FPT/FRT-shaped pattern
being skipped; what changed is the size of the prize:

| | median stock | top decile | best |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | +145.5% | +608.9% | +2,695.6% |
| 2022–2026 | **−18.0%** | +93.4% | +498.7% |

## What DID change

### Q4 — The payoff geometry inverted

| | MFE p50 | MFE p75 | MAE p25 | MAE p50 | MFE/\|MAE\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 5.07% | 12.82% | 1.84% | 4.09% | **1.24** |
| 2022–2026 | 4.24% | **9.33%** | **2.63%** | 4.57% | **0.93** |

The upside tail lost 27%. The ratio crossed below 1.

**Volatility-adjusted, the asymmetry is the whole story** — every trade joined to
its own decision-time ATR:

| | MFE/ATR p50 | MFE/ATR p75 | \|MAE\|/ATR p50 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | **2.03** | 4.77 | 1.60 | 1.27 |
| 2022–2026 | **1.50** | 3.97 | 1.63 | 0.92 |

- **MFE/ATR: −0.64 ATR** (95% CI [−1.40, +0.06])
- **|MAE|/ATR: +0.02 ATR** (95% CI [−0.27, +0.33]) — unchanged

The same pattern, entered the same way, carrying the same relative risk, now goes
against you by exactly as much as before and **in favour by a quarter less**.
Follow-through is what disappeared, not risk control.

*Honest limit:* the MFE/ATR interval grazes zero, so this is the direction the
data supports at n=196/207, not a settled magnitude.

### Q2 — Volatility rose modestly

Index ATR: median 1.29% → 1.40% (+8.5%), p90 2.18% → 2.52% (+15.5%). Stock ATR:
2.67% → 3.12% (+17%). Real but not dramatic, and — per Q4 — it did not translate
into larger adverse excursions in ATR units.

### Q6 — Stops come slightly sooner and more often

| | stop rate | median sessions to stop | within 2 sessions | on entry day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015–2021 | 52.0% | 6 | 28.4% | 6 |
| 2022–2026 | 57.5% | 5 | 32.8% | 6 |

Consistent with less follow-through rather than with tighter stops: stop/ATR on
executed trades is identical (1.73) and MAE/ATR is unchanged, so the extra stop-outs
come from price failing to advance, not from being stopped closer.

Losers also head-fake more: median MFE before failing rose 1.97% → 2.72%.

## Verdict against the ten questions

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market regime changed? | **No** — identical to <0.6pp. And that is the finding: Gate 1 reads the index, which is what did not deteriorate |
| 2 | Volatility changed? | **Modestly** — +8.5% index, +17% stock ATR; not the cause |
| 3 | Breakout→pullback behaviour changed? | **No** in geometry; stop/ATR identical on executed trades |
| 4 | MAE/MFE distribution changed? | **Yes in direction** — MFE/ATR −26%, MAE/ATR unchanged. The interval grazes zero, so direction is supported and magnitude is not established |
| 5 | Entry extension changed? | **No** |
| 6 | Stop-out timing changed? | **Slightly** — 52.0%→57.5%, a consequence of #4, not a cause |
| 7 | Setup frequency/quality changed? | **Somewhat scarcer, unevenly** — but not in the years the strategy lost most |
| 8 | Breadth / index behaviour changed? | **Day-to-day no; cumulatively yes** — 87.4%→41.8% of the fixed cohort positive |
| 9 | Leadership behaviour changed? | **Yes in magnitude** — top decile +608.9%→+93.4%, median stock +145.5%→−18.0% |
| 10 | Winners in an FPT/FRT shape Gate 1 misses? | **No** — leader coverage ~30% in both eras |

**Best-supported cause:** upward drift in the replayable universe stopped
(82% of the same symbols earned less per year). A long-only continuation system
earned drift plus selection; the drift went negative, and the selection component
alone was never shown to be positive — see
[BASELINE-EDGE-VERDICT.md](BASELINE-EDGE-VERDICT.md).

**Mechanism, as far as the data supports it:** favourable excursion weakened
while adverse excursion held. Direction is consistent across every cut; the
magnitude is not pinned down at n≈200 per side.

**Blind spot:** Gate 1 cannot see any of this. It reads one index, and the index
kept rising while the median constituent fell.

## Limits of this analysis

- **Composition.** The tradable universe grows from ~11 symbols/session (2015) to
  ~76 (2022+). The two periods are not the same population; per-opportunity rates
  are used where possible, but the early period skews to large, long-listed names.
- **Survivorship.** 1,182 of 1,537 symbols have no stored bars. Delisted failures
  are absent, so the 2022–2026 median stock return of −18.0% is if anything
  **flattered**.
- **Period lengths differ** (7.0 vs 4.6 years); all cumulative figures are
  annualised where compared.
- **Leader sets differ** — symbols need ≥200 bars within each period, so newly
  listed names enter the second set and not the first.
- The MFE/ATR result is directional at n≈200 per side, not a precise magnitude.
- **The ATR join is sound but worth stating:** the diagnostic run does not enable
  the executable stop floor, so its candidate set is a superset of the v2 trades.
  Every one of the 403 v2 trades matched a candidate on `(symbol, sessionDate)` —
  403/403 — and the ATR in both is computed from the identical bounded decision
  window, so no mismatched population is being compared.
- **Leader returns are ex-post by construction** (full-period realised move). They
  describe what happened, and must not be read as something Gate 1 could have
  known at the time.
